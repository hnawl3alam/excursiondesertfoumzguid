Erg Chigaga (Erg Chegaga): A Practical Guide to Morocco’s Largest Dunes

Erg Chigaga, also spelt Erg Chegaga, is Morocco’s largest sea of dunes and can only be reached by desert track. Here is how to get there, where to sleep and when to go.

Where is Erg Chigaga?

An erg is a sea of sand dunes, as opposed to hamada, the stony desert you cross to reach it. Erg Chigaga (you will also see Erg Chegaga; both are transliterations of the same name) is the biggest in Morocco: roughly 35–40 km from end to end and around 15 km across. Most dunes stand at about 60–80 m, with a few rising higher.

The erg lies in the Drâa basin, about 55 km west of M’Hamid El Ghizlane. On the western side the gateway is Foum Zguid, a small oasis town at the foot of the Jebel Bani on the N12 between Tata and Zagora. Between the two stretches the Lake Iriki plain, the heart of Iriki (Iriqui) National Park.

There is no village and no tarmac at the foot of the dunes, only tracks and camps. That is the appeal for travellers who want something quieter than Merzouga, and the reason you need a 4×4 and a driver who knows the ground.

How to get to Erg Chigaga: via Foum Zguid or M’Hamid

  • Via Foum Zguid, from the west: the track crosses the Lake Iriki plain, a vast lakebed that is dry most of the time. Allow about 2.5–3 hours by 4×4, depending on the state of the track and how often you stop. Foum Zguid is on tarmac from Ouarzazate (about 150 km via Tazenakht), from Tata or Zagora on the N12, and from Agadir via Taroudant and Taliouine.
  • Via M’Hamid El Ghizlane, from the east: the classic approach from the Drâa Valley and Zagora, with roughly 55 km of track to the dunes.

Coming via Foum Zguid, the Lake Iriki crossing is part of the experience, and you can make a loop rather than retrace your route. Our circuits from Zagora and Ouarzazate do exactly that: out via M’Hamid, back across Iriki to Foum Zguid. For the track, stops and timings, see Erg Chigaga from Foum Zguid: route and itineraries. An ordinary hire car gets you as far as Foum Zguid or M’Hamid, and no further.

Erg Chigaga vs Merzouga (Erg Chebbi) – and Erg El Mhazil

Merzouga sits on the edge of Erg Chebbi in eastern Morocco, the country’s most visited dunes. Erg Chigaga is more remote and much larger, although its dunes are generally lower than Erg Chebbi’s tallest. Erg El Mhazil, in the Foum Zguid desert, is a third option: smaller and quieter still.

In short: pick Merzouga if road access and choice of hotels matter most; Chigaga if you will trade tarmac for space; and Erg El Mhazil, the quiet dunes near Foum Zguid, if you want the sand almost to yourself. Our guide to Morocco’s deserts compares more options.

Erg Chigaga desert camps: what to expect

Camps at Erg Chigaga range from basic to well equipped, and every operator uses its own labels. Broadly:

  • Nomad or standard camp: canvas or woven tents, mattresses or beds, blankets, meals in a dining tent or round the fire; washrooms usually shared.
  • Comfort or “luxury” camp: larger tents, proper beds and, at some camps, a private bathroom. It costs more and needs requesting when you book.
  • Mobile camp: on a trek, camp moves every evening, in a tent or under the stars.

Our trips include a night in a nomad tent with dinner by the fire; our treks use mobile camps. Whoever you book with, ask about bedding, toilets, hot water, electricity and winter blankets. We confirm programme and accommodation before you book.

Things to do at Erg Chigaga

  • Sunset and sunrise over the dunes, the highlight of any night in the desert.
  • A camel ride: built into the overnight trips, and an optional extra (about an hour) on the day trip.
  • Climbing a big dune on foot. Soft sand makes it slower than it looks.
  • Evening at camp: dinner, music round the fire and a sky full of stars.
  • Walking in the early morning, or for several days on a desert trek with a camel caravan carrying the kit.
  • On the way, depending on the itinerary: Lake Iriki, a fossil site, the Titanic Iriki, a nomad school, the village of Zaouiat Sidi Abdenabi.

How long? A day trip shows you the dunes but leaves before sunset; one night gives you evening and morning in the sand; two nights add the Titanic Iriki and a nomad school.

Best time to visit Erg Chigaga

The season runs roughly from October to April. Summer is fiercely hot; winter nights are cold.

What to pack for Erg Chigaga

  • A scarf or shesh, sunglasses, sun cream and a hat.
  • A fleece even in autumn; in winter, a warm hat and proper layers for the night.
  • Light closed shoes for the track; sandals or bare feet on the sand.
  • A head torch and a power bank: electricity is limited or absent, depending on the camp.
  • Any medication, some cash and a bag to carry your rubbish out.

Safety: never drive into the dunes alone

Erg Chigaga is remote, which is exactly why you should not go it alone. Never drive into the dunes by yourself: vehicles bog down easily in soft sand, tracks multiply across the plain and the wind wipes out landmarks.

  • Travel with a local driver-guide in a well-maintained 4×4.
  • Carry more water than you think you need and tell someone your route.
  • Don’t rely on mobile signal in the desert.
  • After rain, let the guide choose the track: the bed of Lake Iriki can be waterlogged.
  • Leave no rubbish and ask before photographing people.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to get to Erg Chigaga?

From Foum Zguid, allow about 2.5–3 hours by 4×4 across the Lake Iriki plain, depending on the state of the track and the stops you make. From M’Hamid El Ghizlane there are roughly 55 km of track to the dunes. Starting in Ouarzazate, add about 150 km of road to Foum Zguid.

Erg Chigaga vs Merzouga: which is better?

Neither is better for everyone. Merzouga, on the edge of Erg Chebbi, is reachable by road, has plenty of accommodation and dunes up to about 150 m in places, but it is Morocco’s busiest desert. Erg Chigaga is larger, more remote and far less visited, and only reachable by 4×4. Choose Chigaga if peace and the Lake Iriki crossing appeal more than easy access.

Do I need a 4×4 to reach Erg Chigaga?

Yes. No tarmac road reaches the dunes: from Foum Zguid and from M’Hamid alike, the last stretch is track and sand, so an ordinary hire car stops in town. Even with a 4×4, going without a local driver is a bad idea, because getting stuck in soft sand and finding your way across the plain both take experience of current track conditions.

When is the best time to visit Erg Chigaga?

From October to April. Autumn and spring bring pleasant days and cool nights; in winter the days stay mild but nights can drop close to freezing. Sandstorms can occur in spring. Between May and September, summer temperatures often pass 40 °C, so if you must go then, choose a short trip with an early start.

How much does a night at an Erg Chigaga desert camp cost?

From Foum Zguid, our 2-day / 1-night trip with a night in a nomad tent starts from €150 per person, and the 3-day / 2-night trip with a night at Titanic Iriki from €270. From Zagora, the 2-day circuit starts from €235; from Ouarzazate, from €300; from Agadir, the 3-day, 2-night circuit starts from €420. The exact price is confirmed before you book.

Can you visit Erg Chigaga in a day?

Yes, from Foum Zguid. The day trip, from €125 per person, crosses Iriki National Park, stops for lunch at Zaouiat Sidi Abdenabi, includes tea at the camp and time to climb a dune, then returns across Lake Iriki. It is a long day on the track and you leave before sunset, so a night at the dunes is more relaxed.

Is it Erg Chigaga or Erg Chegaga?

Both. Chigaga and Chegaga are two transliterations of the same name, and you will find both in guidebooks, on maps and on operators’ websites. They always refer to the same great erg between M’Hamid El Ghizlane to the east and Foum Zguid to the west, beyond Lake Iriki.
