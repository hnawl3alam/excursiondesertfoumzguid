Lake Iriki and Iriqui National Park: How to Visit the Dry Lake

Lake Iriki is the vast dry lakebed at the heart of Iriqui National Park, on the desert track between Foum Zguid and Erg Chigaga. Here is how to get there by 4x4, when to go and which trip suits you.

Where is Lake Iriki?

Lake Iriki, often spelt Iriqui, lies at the centre of Iriqui National Park in southern Morocco. The park was set up in 1994 and covers roughly 123,000 hectares between the dry bed of the Draa River and the southern foothills of the Anti-Atlas. It straddles the provinces of Tata and Zagora, and the regional tourist board places it about 140 km north-east of Tata.

The nearest town is Foum Zguid, a small oasis at the foot of the Jebel Bani on the N12 between Tata and Zagora. It is the park’s western gateway: the tracks leave the tarmac just outside town and head out across the plain. Coming from Ouarzazate, it is about 150 km by road via Tazenakht to Foum Zguid.

Most travellers cross the lakebed on their way to the dunes of Erg Chigaga, but it is worth a trip of its own if you like wide-open, empty landscapes.

Is there water in Lake Iriki?

Almost always, no. Lake Iriki is a flat expanse of sand and clay, streaked with salt crust and cracked mud, running for several kilometres. In wet spells it can hold a few centimetres of water for a while and turns into a temporary wetland; restoring that wetland was one of the reasons the park was created.

The lake made international news after exceptional rain in September 2024. On 12 October 2024, CBS News, carrying an Associated Press report, said NASA satellite imagery showed water filling Lake Iriqui, a lakebed described as having been dry for around 50 years. Events like this are rare and the water does not last.

  • Expect a dry lakebed, not a lake full of water.
  • After heavy rain, the lakebed can turn into a mud trap for vehicles and some tracks become impassable.
  • Ask us before you book: we will tell you on WhatsApp what the lake looks like at the time of your trip.

Landscapes, wildlife and plants of Iriqui National Park

Around the lakebed you will find ochre hills, flat-topped mountains and long lines of dunes; the regional tourist board says dunes cover about half the park. In between are wooded steppe and acacia savannah, and some dunes are held in place by tamarisk, a shrub that copes with drought and salty soil.

On hot days the air shimmering over the plain blurs the horizon, and it often looks as if there is water in the distance. That mirage effect is one of the things visitors remember most about Lake Iriki.

What animals live in the park?

Official sources list dorcas gazelle, houbara bustard (a vulnerable species), striped hyena and Barbary sheep, plus reptiles such as monitor lizards, chameleons, geckos and horned vipers. They are shy, so sightings are never guaranteed. When the lake holds some water in winter, migrating birds such as flamingos, coots and geese may stop over.

A few families still live in the park, where the Iriki area remains important grazing land. Please give their camps space and always ask before photographing anyone.

Titanic Iriki: the stop on the lakebed

Titanic Iriki is an auberge out on the Iriki plain, known for its unlikely ship-shaped design, mud-brick décor and shaded terrace. It has become a landmark for travellers crossing the park.

On our Lake Iriki day trip it is the lunch stop. On the Titanic Iriki overnight trip you stay the night, with a Moroccan dinner and the choice of sleeping under the stars or in a traditional mud-brick room. Our three-day Erg Chigaga trip also spends its first night there, after about 60 km of track from Foum Zguid.

Lake Iriki 4x4 tour: getting there

A Lake Iriki 4x4 tour is the only sensible way to visit. The regional tourist board states that a 4x4 is compulsory in the park: the tracks out of Foum Zguid are stony, then you cross sandy stretches and the lakebed itself. Tracks criss-cross and look alike, and mobile signal can drop out, so a local guide who knows current conditions saves time and keeps you from getting stuck in sand or mud. Driving a hire car out here on your own is not a good idea.

  • Start: Foum Zguid, usually in the morning for a day trip or the afternoon for an overnight.
  • Typical stops: salt flats and cracked mud, rock formations and photo viewpoints; some itineraries add a fossil site.
  • On to Erg Chigaga: from Foum Zguid, the track across the Iriki plain takes about 2.5 to 3 hours depending on conditions and stops.
  • Through to M’Hamid: the tourist board allows 5 to 6 hours for the full crossing from Foum Zguid to M’Hamid El Ghizlane.

Best time to visit Lake Iriki

The best months are roughly October to April. In summer, daytime temperatures often top 40 °C; an early-morning outing is possible, but the open plain is hard going in the heat.

Practical tips for Iriqui National Park

  • Pack: sun hat, sunglasses, sunscreen, a scarf against wind and dust, plenty of water and closed shoes.
  • Staying overnight: add a fleece or light down jacket, a head torch and a power bank.
  • Cash: take out what you need in Foum Zguid or before you set off.
  • Leave no trace: take your rubbish with you, stay on existing tracks and leave fossils where they are.

Frequently asked questions

Where is Lake Iriki?

Lake Iriki sits at the centre of Iriqui National Park in southern Morocco, which spans the provinces of Tata and Zagora. The nearest town is Foum Zguid, where 4x4 trips start. The lakebed lies on the desert track from Foum Zguid to the Erg Chigaga dunes, so you can easily combine the two in a single trip.

Is Lake Iriki dry?

Usually, yes. Lake Iriki is normally a dry plain of salt crust and cracked mud. After exceptional rain it can hold a thin layer of water for a while, as happened after the September 2024 storms that were widely reported in international media. Ask us about current conditions before you travel.

Do I need a 4x4 to visit Lake Iriki?

Yes. The regional tourist board says a 4x4 is compulsory in Iriqui National Park because of stony tracks, sandy sections and a lakebed that can turn to mud after rain. A local guide is strongly recommended, as tracks criss-cross, there are few landmarks and mobile signal can drop out.

How much does a Lake Iriki tour cost?

Our Titanic Iriki and Lake Iriki day trip starts from €90 per person, with lunch at Titanic Iriki. The overnight version starts from €140, and the four-day trek in Iriqui National Park from €310. All trips are private and leave from Foum Zguid; we confirm the itinerary and price before you book.

What is Titanic Iriki?

Titanic Iriki is an auberge on the Iriki plain, known for its ship-shaped design, mud-brick décor and shaded terrace. It is a classic lunch stop when crossing the park, and you can also stay the night, either sleeping under the stars or in a traditional mud-brick room.

When is the best time to visit Lake Iriki?

October to April is best, with pleasant daytime temperatures. Winter nights in the desert can drop close to freezing, so bring a warm layer. Summer days are often above 40 °C. After heavy rain, especially in autumn, check the tracks first, because the lakebed can trap vehicles in mud.

Can I combine Lake Iriki with Erg Chigaga?

Yes, and most people do. From Foum Zguid the track crosses the Iriki plain and reaches Erg Chigaga in about 2.5 to 3 hours, depending on conditions. Our Erg Chigaga day trip (from €125) and our two- and three-day desert camps all cross the lakebed on the way.
