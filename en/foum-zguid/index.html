Foum Zguid Travel Guide: Getting There, What to See and Desert Trips

Where is Foum Zguid?

Foum Zguid (sometimes written Foum-Zguid) sits in Morocco’s deep south, in Tata Province and the Souss-Massa region. The town lies at roughly 760 m above sea level beneath the Jebel Bani, the long rocky ridge that runs right across the province. In Arabic the name means “mouth of the Zguid river”.

On a map, look for the N12: Foum Zguid is on that road, with Tata to the west and Zagora to the east, while a separate road heads north to Tazenakht and Ouarzazate. South of town lies Iriki National Park (also spelt Iriqui), which you can only cross on unsealed tracks. The 2004 census counted around 9,600 residents, making it the second-largest settlement in the province after Tata.

Few people come to Foum Zguid for the town alone. It works as a base: you sleep here, stock up, then set off for Lake Iriki, Erg Lmhazil or Erg Chigaga.

How to get to Foum Zguid

There is no airport in Foum Zguid itself. The handiest airports are Ouarzazate, Marrakech and Agadir, and you finish the journey by road. The figures below are approximate published distances; real journey times depend on stops, roadworks and weather.

An ordinary hire car will get you to Foum Zguid. Beyond town it is a different story: the tracks to Lake Iriki and the dunes are unmarked, so you need a 4×4 and a driver who knows the ground. If you are relying on buses or shared grand taxis, ask the day before in Ouarzazate, Zagora or Tata, as timetables change and are rarely online.

Prefer not to drive at all? Several private tours start in a bigger town and pass through Foum Zguid on the way to the dunes: Ouarzazate to Erg Chigaga via Foum Zguid (from €300), the Zagora – Erg Chigaga – Foum Zguid circuit (from €235) and Agadir to Erg Chigaga via Taroudant (3 days, from €420).

Things to do in Foum Zguid

Be realistic: this is a working oasis town, not a sightseeing hub. Half a day is plenty, and it is a pleasant way to slow down before or after the desert.

  • Thursday market: the weekly souk is the busiest moment of the week, liveliest in the morning, when growers and families from the surrounding villages come in to trade.
  • The palm grove: shady paths between the palms make for a gentle late-afternoon walk. Keep to the paths, as the gardens are farmed by local families.
  • The old kasbah: the remains of an earthen kasbah stand on the edge of town. It is a ruin rather than a museum, so look but do not climb.
  • Crafts: an artisan centre opened in 2022 with workshops for carpets, leather and jewellery, and the town has cooperatives making carpets and basketry. Buying here puts money straight into local hands.
  • The main street: grocers, cafés and small shops, and the place to buy water and snacks before you leave the tarmac.

Around Foum Zguid: national park, dunes and oases

The real reasons to come are within a few dozen kilometres:

  • Iriki National Park: created in 1994, it covers about 123,000 hectares between the dry bed of the Drâa and the foothills of the Anti-Atlas. At its heart, Lake Iriki is usually a vast dry lake bed; after exceptional rain it can hold shallow water for a while. The park is less than an hour from town.
  • The dunes: Erg Lmhazil (El Mhazil) is smaller and very quiet, while Erg Chigaga is Morocco’s largest sand sea, about 35–40 km long, roughly 2.5–3 hours away by track.
  • Tissint: an oasis about an hour’s drive towards Tata, known for its salt-water cascades, its palm grove and the house where the explorer Charles de Foucauld stayed. A Martian meteorite that landed near the town in July 2011 bears its name.
  • Allougoum: about 25 km north, an earthen ksar (fortified village) that is being restored, with its mosque, houses and covered lanes.
  • Zaouiat Sidi Abdenabi: a traditional village at the foot of the Jebel Bani, reached by track, with a marabout’s tomb. Several of our trips stop here.
  • Jebel Bani rock art: the area has prehistoric rock engravings and paintings. They are not signposted, so go with a local guide and never touch them.

Practical information: fuel, cash and phone signal

Services in small towns come and go, so we would rather not promise anything we cannot guarantee. Sort these out before you leave the larger towns:

  • Fuel: fill up in Ouarzazate, Tazenakht, Zagora or Tata, and top up in Foum Zguid if a station is open. There is nowhere to refuel once you are on the park tracks.
  • Cash: bring dirhams with you. Do not count on a cash machine, or on paying by card in villages and desert camps.
  • Phone signal: coverage becomes patchy as soon as you leave town. Download offline maps and tell someone at home when you expect to be back in range.
  • Supplies: water, snacks, sun cream and any personal medication are far easier to buy in town than at the edge of the dunes.

Where to stay in Foum Zguid

You will find a handful of hotels, auberges, guesthouses and riads in and around Foum Zguid, plus camps out in the desert. We have not named any here because the options change and the right choice depends on your plans.

  • Early start next morning? Stay in town, close to the meeting point, so you are not driving at dawn.
  • Want the desert on night one? Arrive in the morning, leave by 4×4 in the afternoon and sleep in camp. Most of our two-day trips work this way.
  • After real silence? The Titanic Iriki, Lake Iriki and camp overnight (from €140) puts you out on the Iriki plain, under the stars or in a traditional earthen room.

If you need a room in town over Christmas, New Year or Easter, book ahead and check what it offers for the season: something cool in summer, heating or extra blankets in winter.

Why start a Sahara trip in Foum Zguid?

Most travellers reach Erg Chigaga from M’Hamid El Ghizlane in the east, about 55 km from the dunes. Coming in from Foum Zguid means approaching from the west, which has real advantages:

  • The drive is part of the experience: the track crosses Iriki National Park and the dry lake bed, with mirages and flat-topped hills on the horizon.
  • Fewer people than at Merzouga (Erg Chebbi), the country’s busiest desert near Erfoud. Camps here are more spread out.
  • Several deserts in reach: Erg Lmhazil for peace and quiet, Erg Chigaga for big dunes (most around 60–80 m, some higher), and oases such as Tissint and Laarjimia.
  • Easy to fit into a road trip: the town sits on the Agadir – Zagora and Ouarzazate – Tata routes.

Frequently asked questions

Where is Foum Zguid in Morocco?

Foum Zguid is in southern Morocco, in Tata Province (Souss-Massa region). It lies on the N12 between Tata and Zagora, about 150 km south of Ouarzazate via Tazenakht, at the foot of the Jebel Bani ridge. Directly south is Iriki National Park, with Lake Iriki and, further on, the Erg Chigaga dunes.

How do I get to Foum Zguid from Marrakech?

Drive over the High Atlas via the Tizi n’Tichka pass to Ouarzazate, then continue south through Tazenakht to Foum Zguid. It makes for a long day behind the wheel, so many people spend a night in Ouarzazate. Alternatively, join a private tour from Ouarzazate, Agadir or Zagora that runs through Foum Zguid on its way to the desert.

Is Foum Zguid worth visiting?

Yes, if you are heading into the desert. The town itself is quiet, with a Thursday market, a palm grove, the remains of a kasbah and local crafts. Its real value is location: it is the western gateway to Iriki National Park, Erg Lmhazil and Erg Chigaga, with far fewer visitors than Merzouga.

Is there a cash machine and petrol station in Foum Zguid?

We would rather not promise either, as services in small towns change. The safe approach is to withdraw dirhams and fill the tank in Ouarzazate, Tazenakht, Zagora or Tata before you arrive, then top up in town if you can. There is no fuel or cash anywhere on the tracks of Iriki National Park.

Do I need a 4×4 to reach Lake Iriki and Erg Chigaga?

Yes. Foum Zguid is reached by tarmac, but Lake Iriki and Erg Chigaga are only accessible on unmarked tracks with soft sand and stony sections. A 4×4 and a driver who knows the terrain will keep you from getting stuck or lost. All our trips use a private 4×4 with a local guide.

How many days do I need in Foum Zguid?

One night in town is enough for the market and the palm grove. Add at least one night in the desert so you see sunset and sunrise over the dunes. With three days you can combine Lake Iriki, a night at Titanic Iriki and a night at Erg Chigaga, or set off on a short trek.

When is the best time to visit Foum Zguid?

Roughly October to April. In summer, daytime temperatures often top 40 °C, which makes the desert tracks hard going. Winter days are mild, but desert nights can drop close to freezing, so pack warm layers. Sandstorms can blow up in spring; if they do, your guide will adjust the plan.
