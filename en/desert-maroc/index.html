Which Morocco Desert? Erg Chigaga, Merzouga, Agafay and Foum Zguid Compared

Planning a Morocco desert trip? Here is how Merzouga, Erg Chigaga, Zagora, Agafay and Foum Zguid compare on access, crowds, dunes, season and the time you need.

Quick answer

There is no single Morocco desert. Merzouga (Erg Chebbi) is the best known and easiest to reach; Erg Chigaga, the country’s largest erg, is more remote and reached by 4×4 from M’Hamid or from Foum Zguid across Lake Iriki. Agafay, about 30 km from Marrakech, is a stony desert without big dunes. The best season runs roughly from October to April, and a day trip from Foum Zguid to Erg Chigaga starts from €125 per person.

Erg Chigaga vs Merzouga: which Sahara desert in Morocco?

This is the question most travellers end up asking. Both are true ergs, seas of sand dunes, but the experience differs. Merzouga has hotels and guesthouses along the edge of Erg Chebbi, with many camps on its fringes: easy access and plenty of choice, at the cost of more people. Erg Chigaga is larger and far from any town. You reach it by track, which keeps visitor numbers down.

  • Pick Merzouga if your route takes in Fes or eastern Morocco, or if you would rather avoid desert tracks.
  • Pick Erg Chigaga if you want space, silence and a proper 4×4 crossing, and you are starting from Marrakech, Ouarzazate, Agadir or Zagora.
  • Approach via Foum Zguid if you want to reach Erg Chigaga from the west, through Iriki National Park, rather than via M’Hamid.

Zagora and Agafay: short desert trips, and what to expect

Many tours sold as a “desert trip from Marrakech” go to Agafay or the Zagora area. Both work well when time is tight, as long as you know what you will see.

  • Agafay is a stony desert. It suits quad bikes, sunsets and dinner at a camp, but there are no tall sand dunes.
  • Zagora is a pleasant stop in the palm-lined Draa Valley. The Tinfou dunes south of town are small and busy.

If you want big dunes and a night far from any road, keep going to M’Hamid and Erg Chigaga, or come in from Foum Zguid. Our Zagora to Erg Chigaga and Foum Zguid circuit (2 days, from €235) does exactly that.

Foum Zguid: the quieter western gateway

Foum Zguid (also written Foum-Zguid) is a small oasis town at the foot of the Jebel Bani in Tata province. It opens onto Iriki National Park (Iriqui), created in 1994 and covering about 123,000 hectares. Lake Iriki, at its centre, is a vast dry lake bed most of the time; after exceptional rains it can hold shallow water for a while, as it has in recent years.

The track to Erg Chigaga crosses this plain, taking about 2.5 to 3 hours depending on conditions and stops. Along the way you can include:

  • Titanic Iriki, an auberge on the plain known for its ship-like décor;
  • Erg Lmhazil (El Mhazil), a smaller, quieter dune field that suits walking and camping;
  • the high dunes of Erg Chigaga at the far end.

This route makes sense if you are coming from Agadir, Tata or Ouarzazate and want to stay away from the busiest areas. Every option from the town is listed on our Foum Zguid desert trips page.

What to pack for a desert trip

Hot by day, chilly at night and sand everywhere: a few well-chosen items are all you need.

  • A soft bag rather than a hard suitcase.
  • Light, covering clothes for the day and a fleece or down jacket for the evening.
  • A hat or scarf, sunglasses and sun cream.
  • Closed shoes for the track, sandals for the dunes.
  • A head torch, a power bank and a dry bag to keep sand out of your phone and camera.
  • Any personal medication.

Ask your guide what the camp provides (blankets, toilets, electricity) so you neither overpack nor go without.

The Moroccan desert with children

The desert works well for families if you adjust the pace. Long drives tire children far more than the dunes do, so a nearby starting point such as Foum Zguid cuts down on transfers, and a day trip or a single night at camp is a good way to test the waters before anything longer.

  • Mention your children’s ages when you enquire, so the programme and breaks can be adapted.
  • Bring an extra warm layer for the evening.
  • On several of our programmes the camel ride lasts about an hour; ask whether that suits your children.
  • For older travellers or anyone with limited mobility, talk to us first: the track is bumpy and climbing dunes takes effort.

How to choose a reliable camp and guide

Desert offers often look alike on paper. A few questions help you compare, whichever desert you pick:

  1. Is there a written, step-by-step programme? Times, places, nights and sleeping arrangements.
  2. Is it private or shared? Price and pace differ.
  3. What exactly is included? Meals, camel ride, water, tent or room: get it confirmed in writing.
  4. Who drives and who guides? For Erg Chigaga, a guide who knows the tracks is essential.
  5. What are the payment and cancellation terms? These should be clear before you book.
  6. Where are the reviews? Prefer reviews you can check, such as on Google.

A responsible trip also means taking your rubbish back, staying on the tracks and asking before photographing people.

Frequently asked questions

Which is the best desert in Morocco?

It depends on what you want. Merzouga (Erg Chebbi) has tall dunes that are easy to reach; Erg Chigaga is the largest and most remote erg; Agafay, near Marrakech, is a stony desert. For space and quiet, Erg Chigaga, reached from M’Hamid or from Foum Zguid across Lake Iriki, is often the best choice.

Erg Chigaga or Merzouga: which should I choose?

Choose Merzouga if you are travelling via Fes or eastern Morocco and want easy access, with plenty of accommodation by the dunes. Choose Erg Chigaga if you are starting from Marrakech, Ouarzazate, Agadir or Zagora and prefer a remote place reached by 4×4. Both are genuine dune seas; the real difference is access and crowds.

When is the best time to visit the Sahara desert in Morocco?

Roughly from October to April. Days are pleasant then, though winter nights can drop close to freezing. In summer daytime temperatures are often above 40 °C, which makes walking hard work. In spring, wind and sandstorms are more likely, so pack a scarf and sunglasses.

Are there real sand dunes near Marrakech?

Not really. Agafay, about 30 km south of Marrakech, is a stony desert without big sand dunes. For tall dunes you need to reach Erg Chigaga or Merzouga, which takes at least two days from Marrakech. Our Trek in the heart of the dunes starts from Marrakech or Agadir, from €650.

Do you need a 4×4 to reach Erg Chigaga?

Yes. Erg Chigaga is reached by track, about 55 km from M’Hamid, or across the Lake Iriki plain from Foum Zguid, which takes about 2.5 to 3 hours depending on conditions. Going with a local driver-guide who knows the tracks is strongly advised rather than attempting it in a hire car.
