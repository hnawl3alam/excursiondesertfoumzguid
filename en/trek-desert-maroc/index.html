How a Morocco desert trek works

On a Morocco desert trek you travel on foot from one camp to the next with a guide, while a camel caravan carries the tents, kitchen, water and bags. Each evening camp goes up at the foot of the dunes, dinner is cooked on the spot and you sleep in a tent or out under the stars.

Our treks start in Foum Zguid, on the western edge of Iriqui National Park. The routes link quieter dune fields (Erg El Mhazil, Erg Elhaj Ahmed and Erg El Ghoule), the El Grisim oasis and, on the longer treks, the great dunes of Erg Chigaga, Morocco’s largest erg. A 4x4 usually handles the drive in and out.

Every trek is private: you walk with your own group, on your own dates, and we agree the programme with you before you book.

Sahara trekking in Morocco: day-by-day itineraries

Desert hike and nomad school, 3 days (from €260)

  1. Early-afternoon 4x4 from Foum Zguid through Iriqui National Park; camp at Erg El Mhazil.
  2. About four hours’ walking with the caravan to Erg El Ghoule; night under the stars.
  3. Two hours’ walk to the nomad school, a visit when the families are able to welcome guests, then back by 4x4 across Lake Iriki.

Trek in Iriqui National Park, 4 days (from €310)

  1. 4x4 at 2 pm across the park and Lake Iriki; camp and sunset at Erg El Mhazil.
  2. An easy walk through the dunes with the camels to a new camp.
  3. Walk to a nomad oasis for tea with the nomads, then a night in an earth-built guesthouse at Zouat Sidi Abdnabi.
  4. 4x4 back across Lake Iriki, reaching Foum Zguid by late morning.

Trek in the heart of the dunes, 5 days (from €650)

  1. Drive from Marrakech over the Tizi n’Tichka, or from Agadir, via Foum Zguid to camp at Erg El Mhazil.
  2. Walk to Erg Elhaj Ahmed; camp in the dunes.
  3. Walk to Erg El Ghoule; evening round the fire.
  4. Walk to El Grisim oasis, sand-baked bread and tea; night under the palms.
  5. 4x4 back via Iriki, then transfer to Marrakech or Agadir.

Oasis to Erg Chigaga trek, 6 days (from €650)

  1. Transfer from Agadir or Marrakech; night in a riad in Foum Zguid.
  2. 4x4 across Lake Iriki to El Grisim oasis; camp.
  3. About three hours’ trekking to the Erg Chigaga dunes.
  4. Two to three hours’ walking to a remote set of dunes.
  5. Walk to Erg El Ghoule; tea and sunset.
  6. 4x4 back to Foum Zguid by about 11 am, then transfer.

Agadir to Chigaga trekking, 7 days (from €750)

Six stages are set out: the drive from Agadir to Erg El Mhazil via Taroudant and Taliouine, then walking to Erg Elhaj Ahmed, Erg El Ghoule, El Grisim oasis and the tamarisk dunes, finishing at Erg Chigaga with a sunset climb up the big dune. We confirm the final day’s plan when you book.

How hard is a desert trek in Morocco?

You do not need mountain experience, but this is still a physical trip. Soft sand tires you faster than a footpath, dune climbs are short but steep, and heat or wind add to the effort. The days follow one after another.

  • Suits you if you can comfortably walk two to four hours at a time.
  • Pace: a few hours’ walking a day, adapted to the group, with shady breaks in the hottest hours. Where our itineraries give a figure, it ranges from two to about four hours.
  • Children and older walkers: possible on the shorter treks; talk to us before booking.
  • Health: tell us about any back, knee or heart problems, as camps are a long way from medical help.

Camel trekking in Morocco: the caravan and the terrain

On a camel trek in Morocco the camels are there mainly to carry things: tents, kitchen, water and bags. The cameleer leads and loads them each morning and brings them to the next camp, so you walk with just a daypack. If you would like to ride for part of a stage, ask when you book.

The ground changes from day to day:

  • Dunes of fine sand at Erg El Mhazil and Erg El Ghoule, then Erg Chigaga, where most dunes reach 60–80 m and some are higher.
  • Gravel plains and the vast dry lakebed of Lake Iriki.
  • Acacia steppe and tamarisk country, with firmer ground underfoot.
  • Oases such as El Grisim, with palms and water.

Camp nights, water, food and hygiene

Nights are spent in nomad-style camps, in a tent or under the stars depending on the weather and your preference; some itineraries also include a night in a riad or an earth-built guesthouse. Meals are cooked at camp: breakfast, a shaded midday stop, dinner by the fire and mint tea.

  • Water travels with the caravan. Bring a bottle of at least 1.5 litres and ask us how much is planned per person per day.
  • Hygiene: conditions are basic, with no showers on walking days. Biodegradable wipes and hand gel are useful.
  • Rubbish: leave nothing behind; keep your waste so it leaves with the kit.
  • Diets: let us know about allergies or vegetarian meals in advance.

Kit list for Sahara trekking

  • Light, breathable walking shoes you have already worn in, plus low gaiters for sand.
  • Wide-brimmed hat, sunglasses, sunscreen and lip balm.
  • A cotton scarf or cheche against sun, wind and sand.
  • Light long-sleeved layers for the day; a fleece and down jacket for the evening.
  • A warm sleeping bag in winter: ask us what is provided.
  • Head torch, power bank and a small personal first-aid kit.
  • A soft holdall for the camels and a 20–30 litre daypack.

Best season, private treks and safety

The best season for a desert trek in Morocco is October to April. Days are good for walking, but winter nights can drop close to freezing, and sandstorms can blow up in spring. From May to September daytime temperatures often exceed 40 °C, and we do not recommend multi-day walking then.

We do not publish fixed group departures: every trek is run privately for your group on your dates, at the pace you choose. If you are organising a club, a group of friends or a charity challenge, get in touch to discuss a tailored route. For safety, follow your guide’s instructions, drink regularly, never wander off from camp alone and take out travel insurance that covers medical assistance.

Frequently asked questions

How long is a desert trek in Morocco?

Our treks last from three to seven days: the nomad school hike takes 3 days, the Iriqui National Park trek 4 days, the heart of the dunes trek 5 days, the oasis to Erg Chigaga trek 6 days and the crossing from Agadir 7 days. The drive in and out is by 4x4.

How fit do I need to be for a Sahara trek?

Reasonably fit, but not an athlete. You walk a few hours a day, adapted to the group; where our itineraries give a figure, it ranges from two to about four hours. Soft sand and heat make the effort harder than walking on flat ground, so regular walkers will feel most comfortable.

How much does a Morocco desert trek cost?

Prices start from €260 per person for the three-day nomad school hike, from €310 for the four-day Iriqui trek, from €650 for the five- and six-day treks and from €750 for the seven-day crossing from Agadir. All treks are private, and we confirm the programme and final price before you book.

When is the best time for trekking in the Moroccan Sahara?

October to April. Daytime temperatures are suited to walking, though winter nights can be cold, close to freezing, and sandstorms can occur in spring. Between May and September temperatures often top 40 °C, which makes a multi-day trek exhausting and risky.

Do you ride the camels on a camel trek?

On our treks the camels mainly carry the equipment, water and bags, led by the cameleer, while you walk with a daypack. If you would like to ride for part of a stage, for example if you feel tired, ask when you book so we can arrange it.

Where do you sleep on a desert trek?

In nomad-style desert camps, in a tent or under the stars depending on the weather. Some itineraries add a night in a riad in Foum Zguid or an earth-built guesthouse at Zouat Sidi Abdnabi. Conditions are simple, with meals cooked at camp and no showers on walking days.

Does the trek reach Erg Chigaga?

Two of our treks do: the oasis to Erg Chigaga trek (6 days, from €650) and the Agadir to Chigaga trekking (7 days, from €750), which ends with a sunset climb up the big dune. The shorter treks stay in the quieter dunes of Erg El Mhazil and Erg El Ghoule.
